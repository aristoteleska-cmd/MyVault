# 05 — Monetization

## Pricing

| Tier | Price | Properties | What they get |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Free** | €0 | 1 | Compliance score, fine exposure, list of what's missing, ΤΑΚ calculator |
| **Host** | **€9/mo** or **€89/yr** | 3 | Document dossier, expiry reminders, winter work plan, inspection pack PDF |
| **Manager** | **€29/mo** or **€290/yr** | 15 | Everything, plus portfolio dashboard, owner-facing reports, CSV export |
| **Agency** | **€79/mo** | Unlimited | White-label reports, team seats, priority rule-change alerts |

### Why these numbers

**€9 is anchored against the fine, not against competitors.** The pitch is €108/year
versus a €5,000 first-offence exposure — a 46× ratio that survives any objection. Against
average property revenue of €15,200/year it is 0.7%.

**The free tier deliberately withholds relief, not information.** A free user gets the
full diagnosis: score, fine exposure, exact gaps. They cannot store a single document or
receive a single reminder. This is the right split because the *anxiety* is the marketing
and the *storage plus reminders* is the product. A free tier that gave reminders would
never convert; one that hid the score would never acquire.

**Annual is priced at 10 months** and should be pushed hard in November, when hosts have
just closed the season and are thinking about next year. Annual billing also solves this
product's biggest structural risk, below.

**Manager at €29 is the real revenue line.** 100 managers = €2,900 MRR. 1,000 hosts at €9
= €9,000 MRR but is fifty times the support burden. Sell to managers, let hosts self-serve.

## The structural risk: seasonal churn

**This is the thing that kills this business if you ignore it.** A host who gets compliant
in February has solved their problem. In June they will wonder why they are still paying.

Four defences, in order of effectiveness:

1. **Annual plans.** Push relentlessly. An annual customer cannot churn in June. Target
   60% of paid customers on annual.
2. **Expiring documents.** Insurance, pest control, extinguisher service and first-aid
   contents all renew every 12 months. The product's value literally regenerates each year
   — but only if the reminders keep landing, which is why reminders are paid-only.
3. **ΤΑΚ is monthly.** A declaration deadline every single month is the one thing that
   makes the product relevant in July. This is the strategic reason ΤΑΚ tracking belongs
   in the MVP rather than v2.
4. **Rule changes.** Every time Greek law moves, you email everyone. That is a retention
   event disguised as a compliance update.

Model churn at 4–5% monthly for hosts and 2% for managers. If you cannot get host churn
under 5%, sell annual only.

## Second revenue line: insurance referral

The law now *requires* every one of ~160,000 Greek listings to hold civil liability
insurance, and most hosts have no existing provider. Fakelos knows precisely which
properties lack a policy and exactly when each existing policy expires.

That is close to the perfect referral position: qualified, timed, and trusted.

- Typical Greek liability premium for a rental: **€60–180/year**
- Broker commission: **10–20%**
- Per referral: **€8–35**

If 30% of 2,000 free users need a policy and 20% of those convert, that is 120 policies —
€1,000–4,000 a year at the start, and it scales with the free tier rather than the paid
one. Extend the same model to pest control companies, electricians for the ΥΔΕ, and fire
safety suppliers, all of whom are regional and hungry for lead flow.

**Two rules, non-negotiable:** disclose every commission in the app, and never let
commission influence the compliance verdict. A compliance product that sells you a fake
problem is dead the first time someone notices — and in Greek Facebook groups, someone
will notice within a week.

## Unit economics

**CAC.** Primary channel is Greek Facebook host groups plus content — organic, so CAC is
time not money. Assume €0–5 blended for the first 500 customers. Paid Meta ads targeting
Greek property owners run roughly €15–40 per paid conversion; only turn them on once
organic conversion is proven.

**LTV.**
- Host, €9/mo, 5% monthly churn → 20 months → **€180**
- Host annual, €89, 70% renewal → **~€290**
- Manager, €29/mo, 2% churn → 50 months → **€1,450**

Even at €40 CAC the Host monthly ratio is 4.5:1 and Manager is 36:1. The economics are not
the hard part here — churn and rule-set accuracy are.

## Realistic MRR path

Deliberately conservative. Note the shape: this business grows in winter and flattens in
summer, which is normal and should not be read as failure.

| Month | Season | Free users | Host paid | Manager paid | MRR |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 (Sep) | pre-season close | 150 | 8 | 1 | €101 |
| 3 (Nov) | **winter push** | 600 | 45 | 6 | €579 |
| 6 (Feb) | **peak urgency** | 1,800 | 160 | 22 | €2,078 |
| 9 (May) | season opens | 2,600 | 210 | 34 | €2,876 |
| 12 (Aug) | high season | 3,200 | 225 | 41 | €3,214 |
| 18 (Feb yr2) | **second winter** | 6,500 | 480 | 95 | €7,075 |

**€7k MRR at 18 months** from a €0 no-code stack, one founder, one market. That is not a
venture outcome and it is not meant to be. It is a real business that pays a Greek salary
twice over, built on a legal deadline that is not going away.

The step-change between month 12 and month 18 is the second winter — it is when your
first-year customers hit their annual renewals *and* the next cohort arrives with the same
anxiety. Everything in the launch plan is built to make sure you reach that second winter
with your rule set intact and your reminder engine running.
