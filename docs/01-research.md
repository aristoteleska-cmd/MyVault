# 01 — Research: what actually hurts in Greece during the winter low season

Compiled August 2026 from public sources. Citations at the end of each section.

## 1. The shape of the Greek year

Greek tourism is violently seasonal, and the whole economy downstream of it inherits that
shape. On the Cyclades — Mykonos, Santorini, Paros, Ios — a large share of hotels,
restaurants and tourist-facing businesses simply shut from roughly November to March.
Crete, Rhodes and Athens stay open, but at much lower intensity. The islands run a
business model where roughly six months of revenue must cover twelve months of cost.

Two things follow, and they matter more than the closure itself:

**Winter is the admin season.** Nothing gets renovated, certified, inspected, re-permitted
or re-planned in August — the property is occupied and the owner is working. All of it
gets deferred to the dead months. A Greek tourism operator in January has time, no
revenue, a long list of deferred obligations, and no system for any of it.

**Winter is no longer empty everywhere.** The seasonality is softening: summer's share of
arrivals fell from about 56% to about 50% between 2019 and 2025, with three consecutive
years of double-digit winter growth. Mountain destinations are the clearest case —
Arachova and Parnassos, Pelion, Kalavryta. Kalavryta reported 15,000 visitors a day after
a €22m EU-funded upgrade, and Arachova-area short-term rentals reported 95–100% occupancy
on winter weekends. The 2025/26 ski season ran to 19 April, the longest in years.

So the real winter picture is not "everything is closed." It is a **split**: island
operators sitting on dead inventory with a backlog of paperwork, and mountain operators
suddenly running a real season with tooling designed for neither.

## 2. The regulatory wave hitting short-term rentals

This is the single biggest change in the Greek small-business landscape right now, and it
lands squarely on the winter calendar.

### 2.1 Registration (AMA)

Every property must carry an AMA registration number from AADE's short-term rental
registry before it can be listed on any platform. Enforcement is real, not theoretical:
in Athens, 96% of active listings now show registration evidence.

New AMA registrations are **blocked** through the end of 2026 in central Athens — the 1st,
2nd and 3rd municipal districts, covering Plaka, Koukaki, Kolonaki, Exarcheia and Pagrati.
From 1 March 2026 the same restriction extends into parts of central Thessaloniki, the
first expansion beyond the capital. In these "saturated" zones the AMA is being decoupled
from the property, so it no longer transfers automatically on sale or inheritance — which
changes both investment maths and what a buyer is actually purchasing.

### 2.2 Safety and quality standards — the new pain (Law 5170/2025)

Effective **1 October 2025**, every short-term rental must meet a specific, checkable list:

- Primary-use space only — storage rooms and garages cannot be let
- Minimum ceiling height 2.5 m
- Bedroom natural light from windows ≥ 10% of floor area
- Natural ventilation openings ≥ 5% of floor area
- Air conditioning, except mountain areas above 600 m
- Civil liability insurance from a licensed Greek insurer
- Electrician's declaration confirming a residual-current device
- Valid pest control (disinfestation) certificate from an authorised company
- At least one 6 kg fire extinguisher per 100 m²
- Smoke detectors in bedrooms and kitchens
- Emergency lighting at exit points, and escape signage
- First-aid kit labelled in Greek and English
- Emergency contact list posted visibly

Enforcement is by **joint Ministry of Tourism and AADE inspection with 10 days' notice**.
Fines: **€5,000** first violation, **€10,000** for a second within a year, **€20,000**
thereafter, payable within 15 days before compulsory collection.

Ten days' notice is the detail that makes this a software problem. It is not enough time
to source an electrician, book a pest-control visit and buy compliant extinguishers on a
Greek island in February. The work has to already be done, and the paperwork has to
already be findable.

### 2.3 The Climate Resilience Fee (ΤΑΚ)

A per-night fee the host collects from the guest and remits to the state, with **monthly
declarations due by the last day of the month following** the receipt. Rates jumped in
January 2025 and are unchanged for 2026. For short-term rentals it is €8/night standard
and €15/night for detached houses of 80 m²+ in April–October, dropping to €2 and €4 in
November–March. It is seasonal, property-type dependent, and easy to get wrong in exactly
the months when the rate changes.

### 2.4 Tax and audit pressure

Short-term rental income is taxed at 15–45%. AADE has announced sweeping digital audits
for 2026 targeting both live platform listings and undeclared income from prior years.

## 3. Market size

- Greece passed **130,000 active listings** in 2023 and hit a record **~160,000 in 2025**
  before the usual Q4 dip. Sixth in Europe by volume.
- Athens alone: **12,626 active listings** as of March 2026.
- Average annual income per property in 2025: **€15,200**, at **40.9% occupancy** and a
  **€121** average nightly rate.

That 40.9% occupancy figure is the winter problem stated numerically. And €15,200 of
annual revenue sets the ceiling on what a host will pay for tooling — which is why the
price point in `05-monetization.md` is €9/month and not €99.

## 4. The other winter pressure: myDATA and e-invoicing

Separate from rentals, every Greek business faces the myDATA e-invoicing rollout.
Mandatory B2B e-invoicing began **2 February 2026** for businesses with 2023 revenue over
€1m, and extends to **everyone else on 1 October 2026**, with a transition period to 31
December 2026. Invoices not cleared through myDATA are invalid for VAT deduction on the
recipient's side. Penalties run to 50% of the VAT due on a non-compliant invoice.

This is enormous demand — but it is also the most crowded software category in Greece,
occupied by entrenched accounting vendors and by the accountants themselves, who are the
real decision-makers. Noted here because it shapes the ranking in the next document, not
because it is a good solo entry point.

## 5. What the existing tools do and don't do

| Tool | What it is | Gap it leaves |
|---|---|---|
| Hostaway, Guesty | Global PMS / channel managers | Compliance is a blog post, not a feature; priced for professionals |
| Chekin | Digital guest check-in, myDATA certified invoices | Solves check-in and invoicing, not the physical safety dossier |
| ProofSnap (€9.90/mo) | Monthly screenshots of listing, AMA display, payouts | Evidence capture only; no expiry tracking, no work plan |
| Roomismo | Greek-built PMS with myDATA/ΤΑΚ support | Full PMS — too heavy and too expensive for a one- or two-property host |
| Accountants | The default | Handle tax filings; do not track your extinguisher service date |

Everyone is solving **money paperwork**. Nobody is solving the **physical property
dossier** that Law 5170/2025 just made mandatory and inspectable on 10 days' notice.

## 6. The synthesis that drives the product

1. Greek hosts have **five months of time and no revenue** (Nov–Mar).
2. In that exact window sits **a backlog of newly mandatory, physical, deadline-bearing
   compliance work** that cannot be done in season.
3. Failing it costs **€5,000 on the first offence**, with only 10 days' warning.
4. The obligations **recur annually** — insurance renews, pest control expires,
   extinguishers need servicing — which is the definition of a subscription.
5. The incumbents are all pointed at invoicing and channel management, not at this.

That is the product.
